# unFIX

A pattern library for organisation design, by Jurgen Appelo (the author of Management 3.0). Its own summary: *"like LEGO — there are just a few elementary building blocks of various types, and they are optional, reconfigurable, repeatable, and scalable."*

Use it to give a structure real names, or to find what an existing structure is missing.

## What unFIX is not

Appelo states five negatives explicitly, and each one prevents a specific misuse. Read them before applying anything below.

1. **Not a framework.** "Framework" implies an essential supporting structure; nothing in unFIX is essential. Everything is optional. It is a *pattern library*.
2. **Not a process.** It covers organisation design and structure only. Do not reach for unFIX to decide how work flows — pair it with something that actually models process (Double Diamond, Lean Startup, Team Topologies' interaction modes).
3. **Not IT-only.** No built-in assumption of software development.
4. **Not top-down.** The approach is bottom-up: *"You cannot build something large. You must begin with something small."* Start at one Base.
5. **Not a replacement.** It composes with what exists rather than displacing it.

The Lean principle it follows: **start where you are, and use what you need.**

## The building blocks

### Crew (team, squad, pod, cell)

The unit that does the work. Seven types — the first four are Team Topologies' with unFIX names, the last three are Appelo's additions:

| Crew | Does | Team Topologies name |
|---|---|---|
| **Value Stream Crew** | end-to-end responsibility for one value stream | stream-aligned team |
| **Facilitation Crew** | temporarily helps other Crews get up to speed | enabling team |
| **Capability Crew** | wraps rare or unique skills, lent to Value Stream Crews | complicated-subsystem team |
| **Platform Crew** | offers infrastructure or architecture as if it were a supplier | platform team |
| **Governance Crew** | the Chiefs; where conflicts and trade-offs land | — (from Management 3.0) |
| **Experience Crew** | one representative per Value Stream Crew that touches the customer; owns the whole experience across streams | — |
| **Partnership Crew** | the outside relationships — suppliers, partners, channels | — |

Crews own their roles, methods, objectives, cadence and dependencies. **As long as a Crew takes full responsibility for the value it provides, it has the final say over how it gets its work done.** Appelo prefers "Crew" because the word implies members managing a journey together.

Crews are **stable but not static** — dynamic reteaming (Helfand) is assumed, because *static teams are not agile*.

### Captain (team lead)

**One person carries responsibility for how the whole Crew operates on its journey.** The reason is worth quoting: *"a shared responsibility equals no responsibility."* A Crew may have many leads — Product Lead, Tech Lead, anyone leading in some area, which is fine — but exactly one Captain.

**The Captain never has line-management responsibility.** Appointed or elected; the Base may set rules on who qualifies.

### Base (tribe, business unit)

Where Crews live. A handful to a few hundred people. **The Base acts like a fully grown, independent business** — it contains every skill needed to design, develop and deliver.

**Every person has a Base. It is their home** — purpose, belonging, recognition, safety, shared culture and tools, career paths.

Ideally a Base covers **a customer domain, not a technical domain**. Cadence and synchronisation across Crews are *optional*. Its critical job is to keep reorganising itself as the customer experience demands, because organisational structure is linked to product architecture: **when the architecture needs to change, the organisation needs to change.**

Four Base types, by how tightly Crews couple: **Fully Integrated · Strongly Aligned · Loosely Aligned · Fully Segregated.**

### Chiefs (management team)

Typically Chief Product, Chief Technology, Chief Marketing — sometimes four or five, sometimes divided differently. **The Chiefs are the only people in a Base with line-management responsibility.** Recruitment, compensation, promotions are theirs alone.

The payoff is specific: **the Base keeps a stable reporting structure no matter what happens to the Crews.** People can hop between Crews five times a year without changing manager, and no management territories exist to defend. Chiefs delegate value streams to Captains and functional alignment to Chairs.

**This is what stops unFIX being a matrix.** Two members of one Crew may have different managers — but those managers are always Chiefs on the *same* Governance Crew, one team with one set of objectives. **Every conflict escalates exactly one level.**

### Forum (chapter, guild)

People from various Crews, grouped along functional lines. Named Forum rather than chapter because **its purpose is for like-minded workers to get together, talk, and make decisions together.** Chiefs may delegate standardisation, templates, toolsets, infrastructure, personal development and cross-team coordination to Forums.

**There is no line management in a Forum.** Recruitment, compensation, career development and performance reviews are explicitly outside its mandate — this is the crucial difference from Spotify chapters. **Forums are the connective tissue between Crews.**

Nine types: **Functional · Product · Market · Channel · Business Model · Customer Journey · Regional · Technological · Seasonal.**

### Chair (moderator)

**The Chair is the manager of the Forum and not the manager of the people.** Part-time, usually someone with seniority; manages discussions and decisions, has no say over compensation or promotion. *"First among equals."*

### Turfs

Where a Crew's work physically or virtually happens. Relevant when hybrid and remote arrangements differ across Crews.

## The sixteen Role Attributes

unFIX deliberately does not hand you a fixed role set the way Scrum (Product Owner, Scrum Master) or Holacracy (Circle Lead, Circle Rep, Secretary, Facilitator) do. It hands you ingredients:

**Chief · Captain · Chair · Enabler · Scribe · Envoy · Agent · Guide · Producer · Director · Guru · Creator · Performer · Maven · Custodian · Inspector**

> *"Frameworks give you a specific set of roles. The unFIX Model gives you ingredients to design your own."*

Compose context-specific roles from these. A rotating informal team lead might combine **Captain + Producer**. Someone owning quality might be **Custodian** or **Inspector**. Cross-team dependencies might be handled by **Agent** and **Envoy** rather than expecting everyone to talk to everyone.

**Use few.** Appelo's test is the Italian chef: the kitchen is full of high-quality ingredients, but the plate holds burrata, tomatoes, basil and olive oil. Do not unload half the kitchen onto the plate. Roles buy clarity and cost flexibility; striking that balance is an ongoing judgement, not a one-time design.

## Scaling — fractal, and honestly untested

Everything repeats at higher levels, self-similar across scales:

| Level | Unit | Its Crews | Its coordination |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Base** | Crews | Forums |
| 2 | **League** (dozens of Bases) | Clusters | Assemblies |
| 3 | **Crowd** (dozens of Leagues) | Coalitions | Congresses |

**Report the caveat whenever you use this.** Appelo says so himself: *"most of this upward scaling with unFIX is still hypothetical. We know the patterns work at a small scale; we believe they can work at a large scale too."* Start at the Base.

## How to apply it

1. **Name what exists before proposing anything.** Map current teams onto Crew types. The gaps are usually the finding — a missing Experience Crew shows up as each stream optimising its own slice while the customer's journey across them belongs to nobody; a missing Platform Crew shows up as every Crew rebuilding the same infrastructure.
2. **Check the Base is a customer domain, not a technical one.** A Base drawn around a technology is the classic silo.
3. **Give every Crew exactly one Captain** — and check nobody has confused Captain with manager.
4. **Ask where a conflict goes.** If the answer is more than one level up, or "it depends", the Governance Crew is missing or is not acting as one team.
5. **Pick the fewest Role Attributes that remove real ambiguity.** Name what each buys.
6. **Say what you are not using.** Everything is optional; an explicit "no Partnership Crew, because there are no partners" is a design decision, not an omission.

## Using unFIX for an AI-agent organisation

This is outside Appelo's written scope — he is designing for humans — so separate what transfers from what does not, and say which is which.

**Transfers cleanly**, because it depends on structure rather than on human psychology:

- **Crew types as seat definitions.** Value Stream / Capability / Facilitation / Platform / Governance / Experience / Partnership describe what an agent group is *for*, which is exactly what a seat needs.
- **One Captain per Crew.** "Shared responsibility equals no responsibility" is if anything sharper with agents: parallel writers to one artifact conflict, and a single owner per namespace is the standard fix.
- **Every conflict escalates one level.** A deterministic escalation path is easier to encode than a negotiated one.
- **Forums have no line management.** Cleanly maps to "this group coordinates and decides how work is done, and holds no authority over resources".
- **Role Attributes as composable lenses.** Envoy, Inspector, Custodian, Scribe are better seat names than invented ones, and they come with meanings a reader can look up.

**Degenerates, and needs saying out loud:**

- **Chiefs are defined by recruitment, compensation and promotion.** Agents have none of those. In a one-human organisation every Chief role collapses onto that human, so the Governance Crew is a person, not a team — and the "conflicts land with a team of Chiefs who share objectives" property is lost. State the substitute you are using.
- **The Base as home** — purpose, belonging, recognition, career opportunity — has no analogue. Drop it rather than pretending.
- **Dynamic reteaming** assumes reteaming has a cost worth managing. Spawning an agent is nearly free, so the pattern loses its point.
- **Fractal scaling** is untested for humans and entirely unevidenced for agents. Do not reach for League or Crowd.

**The honest summary to give a user:** unFIX supplies vocabulary and structural discipline for an agent org, not legitimacy. It tells you what each group is for and who owns each decision. It cannot make a group of agents into independent judgement — that comes from giving each seat disjoint inputs, not from naming them.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Treating unFIX as a framework to implement | it looks like SAFe or Scrum from a distance | it is a pattern library; adopt patterns individually |
| Adopting all seven Crew types | completeness feels rigorous | most Bases need two or three |
| Captain as manager | the name suggests authority | Captains have zero line-management responsibility |
| Chair as manager of Forum members | same reason | the Chair manages the Forum, not the people |
| Drawing a Base around a technology | it matches the org chart already there | Bases cover customer domains |
| Reaching for League or Crowd early | scale feels impressive | start at one Base; upward scaling is hypothetical |
| Expecting unFIX to supply process | it is org-design vocabulary | pair it with a process model |
