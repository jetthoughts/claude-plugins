# Lightning Decision Jam

A 30–90 minute workshop that replaces open discussion with structured silence. It exists for one failure: **a group that talks about a problem for an hour and leaves with nothing owned.**

**Problem-first.** Start here when what is broken is unclear. If what is *unclear* is where you are going, you want a goal-first format instead — see the boundary at the end.

**Shape:** 3 people minimum · **4–6 is the sweet spot** · 8 maximum. Low facilitation skill needed. 20 minutes of prep.

## The rule that does the work

**Every generation phase is silent and solo.** This is not a style preference. An open forum lets the loudest person push an idea until everyone else gives in, and the result looks like agreement. Silence removes the mechanism.

**The facilitator's explicit job is to shut discussion down, politely, the moment it starts.** That is the role. Someone who wants to argue the content should not be facilitating.

## The eight steps

| # | Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Set the scope** | 5 min | Name the topic and the time available. No facilitator from outside is required — someone on the team can run it and still contribute, but steering is their main job |
| 2 | **Sailboat** | 15 min | Draw a boat. **Sail above the waterline = what pushes us forward. Anchor below = what holds us back.** Draw it big |
| 2.1 | *Positives first* | 10 min | **4 min silent** writing on stickies. Then each person presents, **1–2 min max**, reading them out. **Clarifying questions only — no discussion, no comment** |
| 2.2 | *Then problems* | 5 min | 4 min silent, as many as possible. Then **everyone sticks simultaneously and silently** onto the anchor |
| 3 | **Prioritise problems** | 3 min | **3 dots each. No cap per item — stack all three if you believe it. Vote for your own if you want.** No discussion. Then sort descending |
| 4 | **Reframe as challenges** | 3 min | The top 3–4 problems become **How Might We** questions. *"The office is too loud"* → *"How might we adjust the noise so we can work well?"* |
| 5 | **Generate solutions** | 6 min | 5 min silent writing. **Quantity over quality** — they do not all have to be usable. Still no talking. *New team? Take only the single biggest challenge* |
| 6 | **Prioritise solutions** | 5 min | **3 dots each**, same rules, silent. Anything with **≥1 dot** survives |
| 7 | **Impact/Effort** | 10 min | Place each survivor on the grid. Four quadrants, each with a prescribed action: **top-left → do it · top-right → make it a project · bottom-left → make it a task · bottom-right → maybe later** |
| 8 | **Make it actionable** | 5 min | Take **only the top-left**. See the completeness test below |
| — | **Outro** | 10 min | Summarise what was produced, check expectations were met, collect feedback on the session |

## Two mechanics worth using everywhere

**Placing an item on the grid without an argument.** The facilitator puts the sticky in the middle, asks the group *"higher or lower?"*, settles where consensus is strongest, then asks *"further left or right?"*. **Two binary questions replace a debate**, and nobody has to defend a position out loud.

**Step 8's completeness test.** An idea is not actionable until it has:

- **one named person** responsible — not a team, a person
- **a deadline**
- **three steps** that would test whether it is feasible
- an implementation window of **one to two weeks**
- **the review date booked in the room**, before anyone leaves

That is a kill criterion arrived at from the opposite direction: instead of asking what would prove this wrong, it asks what would prove it real, by when, and who is on the hook.

## Why the sailboat comes first

Positives before problems, always. **A board of problems gathered without their counterweight reads as a verdict on the team**, and people defend rather than diverge. Ten minutes of what is working buys the honesty of the five minutes that follow.

## What this does not do

**No research, no evidence, no sources.** LDJ runs entirely on what the people in the room already know, and that is deliberate — it is why it fits in an hour. Its output is an owned experiment, not a justified conclusion.

**So do not use it to settle a question of fact.** If the disagreement is about what is true rather than what to do, the room will vote on its shared assumptions and call the result a decision.

## Where LDJ ends and something else begins

| The thing that is unclear | Use |
|---|---|
| **What is holding us back** | **LDJ** — this skill |
| **Where we are going** | a goal-first format: goal + metric, demos, concepts, roadmap, one-pager |
| **What is actually true** | `deliberate` — independent lanes, verified citations, adversarial contest |

**LDJ and goal-first formats are opposite entry points, not competitors.** Pick by which one is genuinely unknown. Reaching for the heavy evidence harness when the team already knows the problem is how a forty-five minute decision becomes a two-day project.

**Run it repeatedly.** The format gets faster with practice, and its value is in the habit — a team that jams monthly beats one that runs a perfect workshop once.
